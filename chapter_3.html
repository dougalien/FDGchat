Abstract

A finite deformable universe model is assembled from the empirical and diagnostic results developed earlier. The redshift analysis treated redshift as accumulated path response, A(d) = ln(1+z), with local response G(d) = g0/(1+d/db), and identified a BAO-CMB shared-ruler failure requiring a sharp projection transition. The observational-register analysis recast that transition into a dimensionless depth coordinate, tested JWST mass-size projection behavior, expressed the SPARC radial-acceleration relation as weak-field compliance, and added matter-void foam and compactness-boundary screens. These pieces define a visual model of a finite deformable geometric domain.

The central proposal is that observed redshift, inferred size, local gravitational response, void architecture, and compactness limits are different observational registers of one finite geometry. The model uses X = A/At as a normalized transition coordinate, D = A G as a raw morphology response coordinate, eta = gbar/gdagger as a weak-field compliance coordinate, and u = 2GM/(Rc^2) as a compactness coordinate. When morphology enters the response engine, D is normalized as D_n = D/D0, where D0 has the same units as D. These registers are collected into a generalized finite-response function C(X, eta, u, D_n), which defines testable relations among projection, gravity, morphology, and compactness.

1. Model premise

The model is intentionally visual, but it remains constrained. A, chi, X, D, eta, and u must remain separate; ordinary local physics must be preserved; and every proposed register must point to a measurable success or failure condition.

Earlier observational audits reorganized real observables under a finite deformable geometry interpretation. The synthesis question is what kind of universe could make the strongest relationships appear as parts of one geometry.

The starting concern is physical infinity. Infinite space, infinite time, singular density, negative time, and negative physical amounts may be useful mathematical limits, but they are not automatically physical structures. A finite event-domain provides the setting in which redshift, gravity, void space, and compactness can appear from inside the domain.

The model is tied to specific empirical patterns: the supernova path-response relation, the BAO-CMB shared-ruler failure, the approximate 0.39 CMB-to-BAO scale ratio, the JWST mass-size direction of effect, the SPARC acceleration transition, the partial matter-void foam result, and the compactness-boundary target.

4. The finite geometric domain

The model treats the universe as a finite deformable event-domain. This does not require a literal hard wall in ordinary space. It means that the event has a bounded geometric register: path response, projection, and compactness approach limiting behavior rather than physical infinity. In the schematic domain notation below, X(x) is a model-assigned depth coordinate for an event or signal path, and Xmax is the maximum depth covered by the chosen model domain.

\[U = \left\{x \mid 0 \le X(x) \le X_{\max}\right\} \tag{3-6}\]

See the Quantitative Appendix for domain-coordinate notation and response-register definitions.

Interior observations have X < 1. The focal projection boundary has X = 1. CMB-side projection behavior lies in a compressed register beyond the transition. Local objects can still sit deep inside the domain while entering a different regime through eta or u. For this reason, the model uses several registers rather than one universal scalar.

5. Path-response redshift

The redshift law is constrained by the earlier redshift-distance analysis. Redshift is treated as accumulated response along a signal path through deformable geometry. The observable redshift is not changed; the interpretation of its accumulation is changed.

\[A(d) = \int_0^d G(s)\,ds \tag{3-7}\]
\[G(d) = \frac{g_0}{1 + d/d_b} \tag{3-8}\]
\[A(d) = g_0 d_b \ln(1 + d/d_b) \tag{3-9}\]

This response law gives a nonlinear redshift-distance curve without requiring local light speed to vary. Light still propagates locally at c. The wavelength, timing, and inferred projection registers accumulate through the geometry crossed by the signal.

6. Projection boundary and one-focal-length normalization

Technical note

The BAO-CMB shared-ruler failure requires a sharp transition. A broad rescaling would disturb the BAO range. The model therefore places the projection transition at one normalized focal length: X = 1.

\[T(X) = 1 - \frac{\Delta X^m}{X^m + 1} \tag{3-11}\]

For the recovery target, Delta = 0.627714 and m = 4. The high-X limit is 1 - Delta = 0.372286, close to the CMB-side compression band and close to e^-1. The scale ratio 0.390906 remains distinct from T(CMB), but both point to a similar compressed-register magnitude.

7. Size and luminosity registers

A projection boundary should affect inferred quantities before it affects direct observables. Spectroscopic redshift, flux, and angular size are measured. Stellar mass, luminosity distance, angular-size distance, physical radius, and compactness are inferred through a conversion. If the projection register shifts, mass and size move in opposite directions.

M_audit approx Phi^2 M_LCDM

R_e,audit approx R_e,LCDM / Phi

This is the central JWST prediction. The model does not need the spectra to be wrong. It predicts that a finite-geometry projection conversion should make some high-redshift galaxies less massive, larger, and less compact than they appear under the standard conversion.

8. Vacuum as a separation between physical and optical registers

A useful way to visualize the model is as a separation between a physical geometric register and an optical or signal register. Earlier exploratory versions of this idea considered a dual expansion picture in which physical expansion outruns the light/signal register. The present finite-deformable formulation does not require ordinary expansion as the fundamental starting point. It preserves the important intuition that a vacuum-like register can emerge from separation between what the geometry is doing and what photon paths can report.

Vacuum is therefore not treated as empty nothing. It is treated as a low-coupling geometric state: a region where matter-linked deformation pressure weakens toward near-zero response rather than becoming a required negative amount. This makes the pressure idea a boundary-state question rather than a negative-energy assumption.

9. Gravity as local compliance

The SPARC radial acceleration relation becomes the cleanest local compliance target. Baryons remain the local source term, while the effective response of geometry becomes more compliant below a characteristic acceleration scale.

\[\eta = \frac{g_{\mathrm{bar}}}{g^\dagger} \tag{3-12}\]
\[C_g(\eta) = 1 - \exp\!\left(-\sqrt{\eta}\right) \tag{3-13}\]
\[\frac{g_{\mathrm{obs}}}{g_{\mathrm{bar}}} = \frac{1}{C_g(\eta)} \tag{3-14}\]

Eta is not the same coordinate as X. A galaxy outskirts can have small X but small eta. A high-redshift path can have large X without being a local low-acceleration system. This separation is a strength of the model because it prevents every observation from being forced onto one axis.

10. Matter-void foam and shape response

The most visual part of the model is the matter-void foam. The cosmic web is not only matter in a background. The voids are part of the geometry. If the model is physically meaningful, void bubbles, walls, filaments, clusters, and local wells should be co-expressions of the same deformable domain.

\[I_{\mathrm{foam}} = \frac{N_{\mathrm{watershed}}}{N_{\mathrm{VoidFinder}}} \tag{3-15}\]
\[\mathbf{S}_{\mathrm{vec}} = \left(I_{\mathrm{foam}}, R_{\mathrm{eff}}, V_e, C_{\mathrm{shape}}\right) \tag{3-16}\]
\[D = A\,G,\quad D_n = D/D_0 \tag{3-17}\]

The SDSS VAST screen showed that D = A G improves several shape metrics relative to A alone, while also showing that the result is mixed and shallow. Dimensional entries in the shape vector are standardized before comparison, and D_n is reserved for response-engine use. The result is a methodology signal. The model prediction is that a deeper catalog sampling closer to X = 1 should show coordinated change in foam architecture: void ellipticity, basin hierarchy, large-void fraction, and connectivity should not vary independently.

See the Quantitative Appendix for D_n normalization and unified response notation.

11. Compactness boundary and finite saturation

Black holes are the strongest compactness test because they push classical coordinates toward horizon and singularity limits. In a finite deformable geometry, the horizon is a compactness-regime boundary beyond which ordinary exterior coordinates are replaced by a finite saturation law.

\[u = \frac{2GM}{R c^2} \tag{3-18}\]
\[S(u) = \frac{u^m}{u^m + u_h^m},\quad u_h \approx 1 \tag{3-19}\]

S(u) is the compactness response law used for finite saturation. Weak compactness leaves ordinary exterior gravity unchanged; compactness near u = 1 activates saturation. A later theory must preserve exterior Schwarzschild/Kerr behavior, shadows, lensing, ringdown observations, and gravitational-wave constraints while avoiding a physical singular endpoint.

13. Field-equation bridge

A conservative bridge to relativistic gravity can be written as an effective correction to the local GR limit:

\[G_{\mu\nu} + \Lambda g_{\mu\nu} + F_{\mu\nu} = \frac{8\pi G}{c^4}T_{\mu\nu} \tag{3-21}\]
\[F_{\mu\nu} = \alpha B_{\mu\nu} C(X,\eta,u,D_n) \tag{3-22}\]

F_mu_nu is introduced as an effective bridge term. The equation requires activation near regime thresholds, vanishing in tested ordinary limits, conservation compatibility, and agreement with the empirical response functions isolated by the observational analyses.

14. What the universe looks like in this model

If the model is physically correct, the universe is not matter expanding through a passive infinite background. It is a bounded deformable geometric event with nested response registers. The low-depth interior preserves ordinary local physics and most SN/BAO behavior. Farther along the path-response coordinate, projection relations begin to shift, producing high-redshift mass-size effects. Near X = 1, distance, wavelength, timing, and standard-ruler registers no longer map through one simple expansion conversion. Beyond that boundary, the CMB-side acoustic projection lies in a compressed register.

Inside the domain, matter and voids are not independent of geometry. Galaxies sit in local deformation wells. Low-acceleration outskirts show increased compliance. Cluster mergers may reveal partial separation among baryonic, lensing, and flow registers. Voids form the foam architecture of the finite geometry. Black holes mark compactness saturation points rather than physical infinities.

16. Model status and required tests

The current formulation specifies T, Phi, C_g, S, G, D, and the response map C(X, eta, u, D_n) as coupled model components. The next step is a unified derivation and numerical implementation that links these components through one conservation-compatible dynamics, including an explicit field-theory form for F_mu_nu and a compactness interior law.

Next tests include a unified numerical model containing X, T(X), Phi, G, D, eta, u, C_g, and S(u); homogeneous JWST mass-size conversion using consistent spectroscopy and morphology; deeper object-level void catalogs closer to X = 1; and SPARC galaxy-by-galaxy finite-compliance fitting.

Register-Specific Scaling Exponents

Within the finite deformable geometry framework, separate observational registers may exhibit characteristic scaling behavior rather than collapsing to a single universal response coefficient. A perturbation-stability analysis was performed using independent model registers derived from the finite response framework.

The tested registers included:

\[D = A\,G\]

for the combined path/morphology response,

\[X = \frac{A}{A_t}\]

for the normalized projection coordinate, and

\[\eta = \frac{g_{\mathrm{bar}}}{g^\dagger}\]

for the weak-field galaxy-response register associated with the SPARC/RAR relation.

Three dimensionless scaling exponents emerged from the analysis:

\[K_D \approx 0.504869\]

for the path/morphology register,

\[K_X \approx 0.980376\]

for the projection/BAO register, and

\[K_\eta \approx 0.666348\]

for the weak-field galaxy-response register.

The resulting values lie near simple fractional forms:

\[K_D \sim \frac{1}{2}, \qquad K_X \sim 1, \qquad K_\eta \sim \frac{2}{3}\]

The weak-field exponent remained particularly stable during perturbation testing.

These results suggest that separate finite-geometry observational registers may organize around distinct scaling exponents rather than a single universal geometric response. In this interpretation, different observational sectors occupy different response regimes while remaining coupled through the broader finite response structure.

Interpretation of the Scaling Exponents

The near-half exponent \(K_D \sim 1/2\) aligns with a familiar geometric-transport pattern. Half-order behavior appears when propagation broadens while local response weakens, as in diffusion-length scaling, random-walk broadening, and other sublinear transport-growth systems. In the present register \(D = A\,G\), accumulated path response \(A\) grows through the domain while the local response factor \(G\) decays with scale, producing a natural setting for square-root-like scaling structure.

The near-linear exponent \(K_X \sim 1\) corresponds to coherent projection mapping across a regime where relative structure is largely preserved. Because \(X = A/A_t\) is a normalized projection coordinate, near-unity scaling indicates that the tested BAO-side projection register retains proportional ordering under the finite response mapping, rather than collapsing into a strongly nonlinear compression in that range.

The near-two-thirds exponent \(K_\eta \sim 2/3\) is also geometrically characteristic. Two-thirds scaling often appears when surface-like and volume-like behaviors are coupled, or when lower-dimensional boundary structure regulates higher-dimensional distributed systems. This includes familiar surface-area/volume relations, packing transitions, and distributed-to-boundary scaling regimes. In the weak-field galaxy register, that behavior is consistent with a distributed response picture of SPARC/RAR-type structure, where baryonic organization and effective geometric compliance co-evolve across the low-acceleration regime [50] [51].

Taken together, \(K_D \sim 1/2\), \(K_X \sim 1\), and \(K_\eta \sim 2/3\) form a low-order rational set associated with transport, projection coherence, and dimensional coupling in geometric systems. Their appearance here is physically interesting because the values emerged from independent observational registers and then aligned as a structured exponent triplet within the same finite-response framework.
