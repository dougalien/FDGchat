A Note from the Author

This work grew out of a personal project pursued in my own time, motivated by a long-standing curiosity about infinity, geometry, and the structure of the universe. I have published in my own field, but this project sits outside my professional discipline and outside the normal purpose of a conventional journal article. It is offered here as a transparent, self-published exploration: a technical web book, a companion guide, an interactive model, and a record of the reasoning that led me here.

The guiding concern is physical infinity. I do not believe infinities should be treated as physical things simply because they are useful mathematical tools. Infinite space, infinite density, negative physical volumes, backward time, parallel worlds, and related abstractions may be mathematically productive, but they do not necessarily describe the world we experience. In this model, infinities are treated as signs that a description has reached the edge of its domain, not as literal features of the universe.

The finite deformable geometry model preserves local tested physics while asking whether cosmological observations can be organized through finite response registers. The model keeps the local structure of General Relativity intact and does not alter quantum theory. The changes appear instead in how redshift, distance, projection, gravity, void structure, and compactness are interpreted on large scales. Local observations remain locally consistent, while far-field observations may require a broader geometric interpretation.

The Hubble tension is one motivation for this work. My concern is not with Hubble’s discovery or with the Hubble constant as an observational measure. The concern is with treating different inferred expansion rates as if they must all belong to one universal expansion scale unless the depth relationship is made physically clear. This book explores another possibility: that the universe may be finite, deformable, and internally measured, with different observational registers remaining linked without collapsing into one global ruler.

Use of AI Tools

AI tools helped make this project possible. They assisted with literature organization, coding, plotting, editing, figure development, and testing ideas that I might otherwise have found too discouraging to pursue. They did not create the work. The questions, assumptions, decisions, corrections, physical interpretation, and final responsibility are mine.

AI is useful only when supervised carefully. It can drift, lose the goal, over-polish language, invent confidence, or undo careful work if given too much freedom. Used carelessly, AI can damage a project. Used carefully, with backups, citations, checks, and clear workflow rules, it can be an extraordinary assistant.

This book and companion app are offered in that spirit: a way to make a difficult idea visible, testable, and easier to think through.
